Mathematical Foundation: Zero-G Capillary Liquid Bridge & Contact Impedance

In orbital vacuum electron-beam additive manufacturing (ISAM), gravity is absent ($g \approx 0$). Without hydrostatic pressure head, the molten pool geometry is governed strictly by surface tension ($\gamma$), electron recoil pressure, and Marangoni flow. The WireArc-ZeroG Auditor samples instantaneous dynamic electrical contact resistance ($R$) and wire feed velocity ($v$), executing real-time inverse reconstruction of the liquid bridge meniscus radius to eliminate void defect formation.

Card 01 Young-Laplace & Zero-G Capillary Force

1. Microgravity Liquid Meniscus Bridge Equilibrium

Without gravity stabilizing the melt puddle, the liquid bridge between the advancing titanium wire tip and the substrate truss is balanced by capillary and recoil forces:

$$\Delta P = \gamma \left( \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \right) + P_{\text{recoil}} = \gamma \kappa(s) + \frac{0.55 P_0}{\sqrt{2\pi \mathcal{R} T}} \exp\left[ -\frac{\Delta H_v}{\mathcal{R} T} \right]$$

Where $\gamma$ is molten Ti-6Al-4V surface tension ($1.65\,\text{N/m}$), $R_1, R_2$ are principal radii of curvature, $P_{\text{recoil}}$ is vapor recoil pressure under high-power electron bombardment, and $\kappa(s)$ is the local meniscus curvature along arc length $s$.

Stability Limit: When bridge length exceeds Rayleigh-Plateau limit ($L > 2\pi r_{\text{bridge}}$), pinch-off occurs.
Card 02 Maxwell Constriction Resistance

2. Dynamic Contact Resistance ($R$) & Necking Area

The high-frequency (100 kHz) sensing circuit treats the liquid bridge as a dynamic constriction resistor whose resistance is inversely proportional to the liquid contact neck radius $a(t)$:

$$R(t) = R_{\text{bulk}} + R_{\text{constriction}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{solid}} L_{\text{stickout}}}{\pi r_w^2} + \frac{\rho_{\text{liquid}}(T)}{2 a(t)} + \frac{\rho_{\text{truss}}}{4 r_{\text{pool}}}$$

Where $\rho_{\text{liquid}} \approx 1.68 \times 10^{-6}\,\Omega\cdot\text{m}$ for molten titanium at 1950 K. If the meniscus begins to neck or droplet detachment begins, the effective radius $a(t) \to 0$, causing $R(t)$ to spike asymptotically.

Microsecond Sensitivity: A 15% reduction in contact bridge neck radius triggers a measurable $3.8\times$ jump in constriction resistance.
Card 03 Real-Time Inversion Algorithm

3. The $(v, R)$ State-Space Inversion & Phase Boundary

Under nominal steady-state wire deposition, wire feed velocity ($v$) precisely supplies volume to match beam melting rate ($\dot{V}_{\text{melt}} = \eta P_{\text{beam}} / \Delta H_m$):

$$R_{\text{nominal}}(v) = R_0 + \alpha \frac{\mu_{\text{eff}} \cdot v}{\gamma_{\text{surface}}} \left( 1 + \text{Ca}^{1/3} \right)^{-1}, \quad \text{where } \text{Ca} = \frac{\mu v}{\gamma}$$

If $R$ increases while $v$ remains constant ($\frac{\partial R}{\partial t}\big|_v > \xi_{\text{threshold}}$), the observer identifies micro-droplet detachment, bead humping, or keyhole void inception without relying on slow optical cameras.

Autonomous Intercept: Repositions electromagnetic focus/deflection coils to broaden pool geometry before droplet separation.
Card 04 Microsecond Deflection Control

4. Microsecond Deflection Control & Sensor Front-End

The embedded controller utilizes a low-voltage isolated AC excitation carrier (100 kHz, 250 mV RMS) passed through the feed guide nozzle into the substrate truss:

$$\mathbf{I}_{\text{deflect}}(t + \Delta t) = \mathbf{K}_p \left( R(t) - R_{\text{target}}(v) \right) + \mathbf{K}_d \frac{d R}{dt} + \mathbf{K}_{\text{feed}} \nabla_{\mathbf{B}} \Phi_{\text{beam}}$$

Sensing: Fast lock-in demodulation ASIC sampling at 100 kHz. Deflection: Quadrupole electromagnetic deflection coils responding in $< 12\,\mu\text{s}$, steering the 60 kV electron beam to melt the retreating meniscus.

Deflection Bandwidth: 25 kHz magnetic steering loop prevents zero-g lack-of-fusion and macro-void entrapment.
